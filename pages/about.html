HOW IT STARTED

A Mill, a Hand-Crank, and a Very Short Supply Chain

In 1948 the founders of Herbs & Spices took a lease on two rooms behind Bazaar Road and set up a hand-crank chakki bought second-hand from a decommissioned rice mill in Palakkad. They ground for the spice traders on the lane, and ground slowly, because the stones were old and the bearings were worn.

What they did not do was blend to cheapen, extend or cut. Traders brought clean pepper, they ground it, and it went back out in sacks. Decades later the atelier is climate-controlled and the grind is measured to a micron — but the deal with the customer has not changed. You bring the spice, we do not adulterate it, and the batch card tells you who grew it.

Master miller Ravi runs the stones now. Lakshmi Menon runs the contracts with the 2,400 farming families the store buys from, most of them in Salem, Guntur, Ramganj Mandi, Unjha and the Idukki highlands.

  • No brokers in the chain. We buy direct, and we pay the same day the lot is accepted.
  • Every pack carries its batch card. District, grower, harvest window, grind date and measured temperature.
  • Decisions are logged. If a run goes over 38 °C it is reground, not shipped.
